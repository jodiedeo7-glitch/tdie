# REPORT: what precedes fast growth for small Threads accounts (Job 13, Part 1)

Written 27 September 2026 for Jodie DeOliveira, The Digital Income Edit™. Companion files: `ACCOUNTS.csv` (162 data points across 50 accounts and 3 dataset sources, each labelled and linked) and `EVIDENCE.md` (post-by-post notes).

## 1. Read this first: how much to trust this

- **Method.** Nine parallel search agents plus direct searches, about 230 queries in all, built from web search results only. The session's network policy blocked every page fetch (threads.com, instagram.com, medium.com, substack.com, buffer.com, blacktwist.app, dev.to, gumroad.com, skool.com, podcast hosts). Threads exposes post text as the page title of the post URL, so search results returned many posts verbatim, plus display names, bios and follower counts as indexed. A shared search cap cut every agent short of its query target, so coverage is uneven.
- **Labels.** VERIFIED = text seen in a search excerpt of that exact page, with link. REPORTED = the creator or a third party says so, with link. INFERRED = my reasoning, with the reason. Nothing INFERRED is presented as fact. Approximate post dates come from Threads post-ID ordering and may be off by a month.
- **What was never visible.** Per-post likes, replies, reposts and views (unless the creator stated them), posting times, images, reply threads, pinned posts, profile pictures, account creation dates. Jodie's own account (@nursemadedigital) is not indexed and the page is blocked, so there is no baseline for her.
- **Two biases run through the whole set.** (1) Almost every fast grower found in Jodie's exact niche sells a Threads-growth product, so their numbers are also their marketing. (2) The only platform-level datasets (BlackTwist, Buffer) are samples of those vendors' own users, not Threads at large, and they measure correlation, not cause.
- **Count of accounts.** 50 accounts logged (32 in Jodie's niche or its immediate neighbours, 18 adjacent or calibration), 24 with a documented jump, 12 counter-examples or slow growers, plus 6 in-niche baseline accounts that sit where Jodie's account sits.

## 2. The five findings that matter most

**Finding 1. Conversation is the most repeated signal, at account level and in every dataset.** 9 of 24 jump accounts show a reply or conversation behaviour at the jump (Gladys overwhelmed by comments; Lennox Saint using replies as a data signal; BlackTwist naming @melodijoelperez as a "replies" case; De Vita replying all weekend to readers who posted their own first lines; Kumar's 20 to 30 accounts a day and five-exchange conversations; Richlyvault's "every follow came from real interaction"; Naji's "invite connection"; the McNay museum's public back-and-forth; Tadeas's 50 comments a day, which is advice not a log). Every BlackTwist month (Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul 2026) reports replies received as the strongest growth correlate (July: median growth 2.90% under 10 replies rising to 19.00% at 500+). Buffer's 128,000-post regression finds replying to comments lifts a post's engagement about 42%. Counter-evidence: Annisy grew 0 to 7K "without engaging 50 times a day", and a two-week-old account got 143 replies on 9,150 views and only nine followers, so replies on a brand-new account did not buy followers. Reading: replies are the strongest thing in the record, but they measure a conversation already happening; nobody in the set demonstrated that manufacturing replies on a cold account starts one.

**Finding 2. The post that grows is usually about the creator, in the first person, with a specific number or identity in the first line.** 9 of 24 jump accounts have their growth-window posts open with their own numbers or milestones (Mirella's "51K followers, 23M+ views ... in 11 months", Naji's "20 months ago I jumped on Threads with 52 followers", Taz's "14 million readers in 30 days", Annisy's "I hit 7k", Palmer, yourwealthmate, ilanashamir, hirah, Tadeas), and 8 open with an identity or origin line (Taz "I'm a 46 year old teacher", "I was a burnt out 46 year old teacher, 11 followers"; shygirlmoney "I'm a 36 year old stay at home mom. In 22 months..."; Naji's 500-euro room). Taz's earliest reach event (30K views, about Oct 2024, on a tiny account) is one she credits to a first-person "I WAS..." opener, VERIFIED in her own post. Naji's six structures from auditing his viral posts are personal updates, milestones, transformations, calling out a specific audience, lessons, controversy. Caveat: these are the posts search engines index, so the count is inflated by what is visible, and "results posts" are a marketing format. But the identity-opener pattern also shows in the non-selling cases (Taz in 2024, ilanashamir, De Vita).

**Finding 3. Most in-niche jumps came after a long slow stretch, and the creators say the slow stretch was daily posting.** 7 of the jump accounts posted for six months or more at small size before the jump: Taz (about 16 months from 11 followers), Lennox Saint (10 months, 17 to 3,800), @melodijoelperez (at least 13 months under 2,400 then +26,900 in March 2026), Naji (10 months to first money; later a stall at 50K), Tadeas (8 to 9 months to 10K), Mirella (six slow months, 15.6K to 24.7K, before the fast stretch), Gladys (100 consecutive days, "almost ditched Threads", then one post: 51K views, 351 followers, 102 lead downloads in 24 hours). Four documented counter-examples show daily posting alone producing little: Buffer 267 to 290 in 30 days; Tami Oladipo 366 to 824 in three months of one scheduled post a day plus 20 minutes' engagement, then she left for Instagram; Bloom Stories' two-week daily push in Jan 2026 failed; Gladys's own 100 days. The fast-from-zero cases (7: yourwealthmate 14 days, ilanashamir 20 days, hirah one month, Kumar 45 days, Richlyvault 72 hours, Kira Goldberg 90 days, the AI-art-prompts writer 72 hours) are mostly self-reported by people selling something, in adjacent niches, or under 2,000 followers. Reading: for Jodie's niche the realistic shape is a long floor then a step, not a ramp; frequency is a floor condition, not the trigger (only Naji describes raising frequency as the change, and he raised it to 4 to 5 short posts a day).

**Finding 4. Narrowing the topic and rewriting the bio to match is the change most often named by the credible non-sellers.** 6 jump accounts show it: Lennox Saint's method after ten slow months was to pull his 30 best posts, sort them into topics, keep the top three, rewrite the bio to those three and post one topic a day; Taz credits "clearly communicating what she provides in her bio" and a three-bucket writing system for her 5,000-follower week, and her display name changed three times around it; Palmer's "one niche, one promise, one audience"; hirah's "Do you have a specific Niche or are you lost?"; Kumar's one lane; Rosette Wilson's bio names her reader and the feeling ("For women in the marriage nobody warned you about"). Tami Oladipo's post-mortem after not reaching 1,000 was "narrow the content focus". Bio or name change is VERIFIED at only two accounts (Taz, Lennox), so the count is small, but it is the cleanest before-and-after in the record.

**Finding 5. Long numbered threads win reach; short posts win engagement; links and offers did not stop anyone growing.** 8 jump accounts used long numbered list threads in their growth window (Taz's "14 brutal truths", Tadeas's lists, @melodijoelperez's tool lists with links, lintropreneur's French numbered lists, Lennox's 13 Hormozi lessons, Mirella's three-step systems, Palmer, yourwealthmate). BlackTwist finds long-form threads the highest-views format in every month reported (July: 12,899 average views, about 18 times a short post), while short posts have the highest engagement rate (March 6.09%, July 7.84%) and, in the July data, text-only posts out-reached posts with media 6,259 to 2,501. Naji's jump came from 4 to 5 short posts a day; Annisy grew on "short and readable". Both formats appear at jumps; nothing in the record separates them. On links: 9 growing accounts had a raw link, a product name or a lead magnet inside their growth posts (Mirella, Naji, Tadeas, Gladys's 102 downloads, Lennox's Skool links, @melodijoelperez's newsletter links in replies, Palmer, hirah's DM keyword, Taz's shop link), and Mosseri's own Nov 2024 statement is "We don't downrank links, but we don't place much value on p(click) and people don't like and comment on links much." So a link costs conversation, not distribution.

## 4. Ranked triggers

Ranked by how often they appear times how good the evidence is. Rank 1 is the most supported, not the most certain.

1. **A conversation on the post** (replies both ways, readers contributing their own material). Most repeated, in accounts and in every dataset. Cause unproven; cold-account replies did not convert in the one test.
2. **A first-person post with a specific identity or number in line one** (who you were, what happened, in figures). Repeated across sellers and non-sellers; Taz's 2024 case is the one VERIFIED before-and-after on a tiny account.
3. **Narrowing to one topic and rewriting the bio to say it** (Lennox's data method, Taz's bio credit, Tami's post-mortem). Small count, best-quality sources.
4. **Long numbered threads for reach, short plain posts for engagement, both daily.** Reach is well supported; follower conversion is not measured anywhere.
5. **Time on the floor.** Six months to a year of daily posting preceded most in-niche jumps. Not a trigger, but the pattern says the jump rarely came in month one for accounts like Jodie's.
6. **Personal struggle and transformation content** (burnout, the broke months, rough patches). Moderate count.
7. **Something to download or reply for, inside the post that reaches** (Gladys's 102 leads, hirah's "learn", the Skool and Gumroad links). Well supported that it does not hurt reach; it is what turned reach into a list for Gladys.
8. **Another account amplifying you.** Three thin cases, no data. Plausible, unproven.
9. **Buying the method.** Three testimonials, all commercial.
10. **Cross-posting, video, posting times.** Weak or contradictory.

## 5. Counter-examples, named

- **Daily posting without growth:** Buffer 267 to 290 in 30 days; Tami Oladipo (Buffer) 366 to 824 in three months, then left for Instagram; Bloom Stories' Jan 2026 two-week push; Gladys's 100 days before her one viral post; Naji's weeks of stall at 50K while posting daily.
- **Reach without followers:** SkillChirp 159K views, 255 followers; Founder Marketing Lab 9,150 views, 9 followers; a printables starter 7,000 views, 26 followers in week one; Krumholtz 1.9M profile views, 6,000 followers (0.3%).
- **A viral post that did not compound:** De Vita, two 20,000-view posts in Sep 2024, 933 followers two years later; ilanashamir 15 to 1K in 20 days then "stuck" at five months; @melodijoelperez stopped at 30.4K after the March jump.
- **In-niche accounts posting the same faceless-mom content and staying small:** Abbi 474, ICU nurse Christina 59, @sahm_inspo 274, @hustlin_mama_ 653, @ariyahdigital 1.4K, @anila_digitalhub 28. This is Jodie's exact field and it is crowded with near-identical bios; two 2024 to 2025 posts mock it directly ("No one has a stronger opinion than a faceless threads profile with 12 followers"; "The number 1 trend that I hope ends in 2025: 'Faceless' marketing").
- **The growth-course sellers' numbers do not reconcile with themselves:** Kira Goldberg's "4,000 in 90 days" (May 2024) against "32K in 3 months" on her sales page; Mirella's views figures; Tadeas's 25K claim against a 17K to 19.5K profile.

## 6. Where the evidence is thin

- No post-level data (likes, replies, views) for any account except where the creator said a number.
- The two best third-party-measured in-niche jumps (@melodijoelperez, @rosettewilson_) have no visible posts from the growth month.
- No dataset on big-account engagement, quote posts, reposts, polls, topic tags or the first hour.
- Timing and video findings contradict across datasets; no account changed its timing and then grew.
- Everything from Medium, Substack, Buffer and BlackTwist is an excerpt of a page, not the page. BlackTwist's January frequency figures contradict each other inside the excerpt.
- Nothing rules out paid promotion or engagement groups behind any claimed jump; nobody discloses either.
- Jodie's own numbers were not reachable, so nothing here is benchmarked against her account.

## 7. What this means for a small account (plain English)

1. **Expect a floor, then a step.** In this niche the accounts that jumped had posted daily for six to sixteen months at a few hundred or a few thousand followers first. Plan for that, and measure the floor by replies and lead downloads, not followers.
2. **The post that moves you is about you, in the first person, with a real number or a real identity in the first line.** "I was a burnt-out nurse with 11 followers" is the shape that reached on tiny accounts. Results posts only work once there are results; identity posts work from day one.
3. **Make the post something people can answer with their own material.** The clearest small-account viral (De Vita) was a post readers could reply to with their own first lines. Kumar's open questions beat statements three to one on his account.
4. **Be in the replies the day it moves.** Every creator who described a good day described a weekend of replying. Replies received are the one number every dataset ties to growth.
5. **Say one thing and put it in the bio.** The two cleanest before-and-afters (Lennox, Taz) both narrowed to a few topics from their own data and rewrote the bio to match. Tami's failure post-mortem says the same.
6. **Long numbered threads for reach, short plain posts for conversation, both, daily.** No evidence favours one over the other for followers; the datasets say long gets views and short gets replies.
7. **Put the thing to download inside the post that reaches.** Links did not hurt reach in any case seen, and Gladys turned one post into 102 downloads. A link costs replies, so a reply keyword (hirah's "learn") is the observed alternative.
8. **Posting more is not the fix when nothing is moving.** Gladys, Buffer, Tami and Bloom Stories all tested it. Naji's fix was more posts, but shorter and about himself.
9. **Expect the step to stop.** Every documented jump plateaued when posting slowed or the format ran out. Taz lost 34 followers in one week off.
10. **Treat every "0 to 30K in 90 days" as an ad.** In this niche the fast-from-zero stories come from people selling the method, and their own numbers do not reconcile.
