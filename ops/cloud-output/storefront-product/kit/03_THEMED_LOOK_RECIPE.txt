AUTOMATION 1: THE THEMED-LOOK RECIPE
The weekly task reads this file, MY_RECIPE.txt and 12_STATE_SCHEMA.md at the start of every run, and follows the Automation Rules in 12_STATE_SCHEMA.md Part A for every step. You never edit this file. Your theme, boards, times and path live in MY_RECIPE.txt.

WHAT ONE LOOK PRODUCES
One look = 5 to 8 Amazon pieces that go together (a roundup: 3 or 5), one list or page that links every piece, and the number of Pins saved in PIN PACKAGE / PINS PER LOOK.

BASIC = 2 Pins:
- Pin 1: product edit / flat lay
- Pin 2: lifestyle / in-use

EXPANDED = 3 Pins:
- Pin 1: clean/product-focused flat lay
- Pin 2: styled/editorial flat lay
- Pin 3: lifestyle / in-use

Pins from the same look are staggered across separate publishing days whenever capacity allows. Production and publishing cadence are separate.

Three kinds of look, all built the same way:
  Outfit: one full outfit. Main piece (dress, set, or top plus bottom), shoes, bag, at least one piece of jewelry, one accessory, and optionally one beauty item or one book (a plain clothbound book with no title showing, named by its kind, never its title or author).
  Theme list: 5 to 8 matching finds for one space or purpose (a room, a desk, a car, a party table, a gift guide).
  Roundup: 3 or 5 options of one item ("3 cozy cardigans for...").

PART 1. THE RULES THAT NEVER MOVE
====================================================================

R1. Publishing is handled by the selected publishing path in MY_RECIPE.txt. The recipe does not use Pinterest scraping, Pinterest internal endpoints, or browser automation to simulate Pinterest publishing.
R2. The TIME-SAVING path uses the customer's connected Metricool account when the current ChatGPT + Metricool workflow is available and has passed the customer's test publish. The CREDIT-SAVING path prepares the complete Pin package for the customer to schedule through Pinterest's native scheduler.
R3. Every pin description starts with "#ad " and ends with the DISCLOSURE LINE from MY_RECIPE.txt ("As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."), so #ad appears once, at the start. A pin with a link and no disclosure, or a disclosure and no link, does not ship.
R4. No prices, anywhere. Not on the image, title, description, alt text, site page, look idea, or item name.
R5. No third-party IP in any image. No film stills, character likenesses, logos, designer monograms, team or university crests, printed brand names, book titles, or other protected identifiers.
R6. No brand names in listings, Pin titles, descriptions, hashtags, alt text or page item names. Describe the item plainly. No real people, films, shows or characters in customer-facing content.
R7. Pins from one look are staggered according to PIN PACKAGE and the saved slot map. Prefer separate, nonconsecutive days for sibling Pins when capacity allows. Never publish multiple near-sibling Pins from the same LOOK_ID back-to-back.
R8. No affiliate links in email. If you ever email about a look, link to your own site page, never directly to Amazon.
R9. Do not use Amazon-hosted product images as published assets. They are not uploaded as generator references. Use original generated imagery or customer-owned/licensed imagery.
R10. At most 4 image-generation attempts per final asset: initial generation, up to two in-place corrections, and one fresh retry with the correction incorporated. If still wrong, drop the look and log why.
R11. Verify every required destination before a Pin enters the publishing queue. For the Influencer path, the look's own Idea List must open and hold the look's pieces (verified through the tested operator; customer confirmation is reserved for a chosen manual step). For Associates-only, the site page must open before publishing. The destination's domain must have passed the QUICK DESTINATION TEST in 08_STOREFRONT_PATHS.txt. If Pinterest blocks a domain, no Pin may point there, and never disguise or redirect a link to get around a block. Never point a Pin at a general storefront home page while its copy says every piece is linked: the link must hold the pieces the copy names.
R12. Never schedule more than 14 days ahead in the storefront system, even though Pinterest and Metricool may support longer horizons. On native Pinterest, never hand me more Pins to schedule than fit Pinterest's limit of 10 future Pins; hold the rest as drafts and add them as earlier Pins go live.
R13. If Pinterest exposes an AI-generated/AI-modified control in the selected publishing path, enable it when appropriate to the actual asset.
R14. Amazon affiliate links must come from the customer's authorized Amazon workflow. Never invent, alter or substitute a customer's affiliate tag.
R15. No em dashes in customer-generated copy.
R16. State is authoritative. Stable LOOK_ID and PIN_ID values prevent duplicate work. Human-readable markdown exports are views of state, not the source of truth.
R17. Do not silently switch image models, providers, or paid plans when a credit or access limit is reached. Record the waiting state and tell the customer what is required.
R18. Amazon product research must respect Amazon's current terms and the customer's authorized access. Do not scrape Amazon in the background or bypass access controls.

R19. Every automated or credit-heavy step must have a lower-cost/manual equivalent. The customer may use ChatGPT or Claude for planning/copy, run the selected image provider directly, schedule natively in Pinterest, and update the state/log manually. The approval, disclosure, destination and duplicate-prevention rules are identical in every path.

LOW-COST / MANUAL EQUIVALENTS
- Product intake: customer supplies the product rows and authorized links directly.
- Image generation: ChatGPT or Claude writes the prompt; customer runs the chosen image provider directly and returns the asset for QA.

Never make a paid connected action mandatory when the manual equivalent can complete the same customer-controlled step.

PART 5. WRITE THE PIN COPY
====================================================================

Title, 60 to 100 characters: the search phrase first, then the hook. Specific, a little flirty. Example: "Cozy Fall Outfit Ideas for Moms: The Sweater and Boots Look for Every School Run".
Description, 450 to 500 characters including the disclosure: start with "#ad ", then a first sentence that repeats the search phrase naturally. Name what's in the look (by description, never brand). One line of personality in my VOICE from MY_RECIPE.txt. Then use destination-accurate wording: "Shop the linked edit" for an inspiration image with similar items; "Every featured product is linked" only when all featured products are identified on that destination. Exclude scene props from product claims. A storefront-home fallback must say it does not identify the exact pieces shown. Then 3 to 5 keyword hashtags (the #ad doesn't count). Then the DISCLOSURE LINE, last.
Alt text, 150 to 200 characters: a plain description of what's literally in the image, including the title words if they're on it.
Board: the board in MY_RECIPE.txt that fits the look. If the board has sections, use the one MY_RECIPE.txt names for that kind of look; if none fits, no section.
No price. No brand. No em dashes. No promise about what the buyer will get or how fast.
Write each pin's full copy into pin-drafts.md the moment it's finished.
