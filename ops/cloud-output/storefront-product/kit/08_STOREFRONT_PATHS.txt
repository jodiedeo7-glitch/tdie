STOREFRONT PATHS
Where your pins send people. Your setup chat picked one from your answers to question 3.

====================================================================
THE AMAZON INFLUENCER PATH (Idea Lists): the easy one
====================================================================

You need: Amazon Influencer approval and a storefront (amazon.com/shop/yourname). Amazon approves Influencers from certain social accounts; check Amazon's current Influencer page for which ones count.

How it works: every look gets its own Idea List in your storefront, holding every piece. Both pins of the look link to that list. Someone taps the pin, lands on a list of exactly the pieces she saw, and a qualifying purchase through it is tracked to your Associates tag.

The recipe builds the list for you (03_THEMED_LOOK_RECIPE.txt, Part 3 step 5) and checks it loads before any pin links to it.

Fallback: if a list truly can't be made or won't load (a piece won't add, the editor won't save, the list is still in review), the pins link to your storefront home and the log says why. Never a single-product link. Never a list for a different look.

No website needed. Nothing to paste. This is the path where "no building, no selling, no posting" is exactly true.

====================================================================
THE ASSOCIATES-ONLY PATH (your own site page)
====================================================================

You need: an approved Amazon Associates account, and a website where a shop-the-look page can live.

How it works: every look gets a page on your own site, with the look's name, a short intro, the two pin images and every piece linked with your SiteStripe short link. Both pins link to that page. Every link on the page carries rel="sponsored nofollow", and the page carries the disclosure "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." Link and disclosure ship together.

PAGE GATE: A Pin may not enter PUBLISHING or SCHEDULED unless its destination page has been verified live in the same content cycle. Store page_url and page_verified_at in storefront-state.json. If the page cannot be opened and verified, set the look/page state to PAGE_PENDING or ACTION_REQUIRED and leave the Pin in READY, never SCHEDULED. Maintenance may re-check a pending page, but it must not publish the Pin until page verification succeeds. If the page was already verified and its URL has not changed, the stored verification may be reused within the configured validity window; otherwise re-verify.

TWO KINDS OF SITE

1. A site the tasks can publish to (a Weekend Ecosystem™ site on GitHub): hands-off. 09_THE_BLOG_HALF.txt adds the section in one paste, and the weekly task publishes each look's page before its pins.

2. Any other site builder (Squarespace, Wix, WordPress, Shopify and the rest): the task can't publish there, so this is hand-work, and I'd rather you know that before you choose it. For each look the task writes one file into your folder, pages/<pin 1 date>-<slug>.txt, and you paste it in: about 10 to 15 minutes a look. The file holds, in this order:
   - the deadline to paste it (12:00 pm the day before its pin 1)
   - where it goes (a new page or blog post under SITE PAGE BASE)
   - the page title and the URL slug (the look name, lowercase, hyphens, no dates); the page address is SITE PAGE BASE plus the slug, and it's the link on both pins
   - a meta description of 155 characters or fewer
   - the two images to add and their alt text: open your Higgsfield (and Gemini) history, find the two images by the date and prompt words written in the file, and save them yourself (the tasks never download anything, so this one is yours)
   - an 80 to 150 word intro in your voice, no prices
   - the numbered item list as ready-to-paste HTML, every link with target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener"
   - the line "The images on this page are AI-generated styling photos. The linked pieces may look slightly different in real life."
   - the disclosure line
   Paste it, publish it, and the maintenance task verifies the page and schedules the eligible Pins.

BEFORE THE FIRST PIN (your setup chat asks you to do both)
- In Associates Central, Account Settings, your website list includes your site and your Pinterest profile.
- On Pinterest, start a pin, paste your site's address into the link field, and check Pinterest accepts it. If it refuses, your domain is blocked on Pinterest and this path can't run until that's fixed.

====================================================================
BOTH PATHS
====================================================================

Amazon needs every place your links appear listed in your Associates account (Account Settings, websites and mobile apps): your Pinterest profile, and your website if you have one.

New Associates accounts: Amazon has had a first-sales requirement for new accounts. Check the current rule in Associates Central. Pinterest is slow, so if your account is new, don't count on pins alone to meet it.

Disclosure on every pin, both paths: "#ad" at the start of the description and "#ad As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." at the end.

No prices anywhere. No brand names in list titles, page titles or item names. Amazon's own product photos are never posted: they're references for the image generators, nothing more. And never an affiliate link in an email (Amazon's rule): if you ever email about a look, link to your own page.
