## 1. First-5-seconds test

| Question | What a stranger gets in 5 seconds | Why |

| What is TDIE? | "An online-business course/community." | The hero image says "roadmap to build your online business." Nothing yet says *what kind* of business (digital products, faceless, AI-built). |

**Diagnosis:** the *body copy* passes the 5-second test. The *hero image* doesn't: it leads with a generic promise and hides the two differentiators (backwards, faceless with AI) in its smallest type. The body's first line then sends the eye to a price increase before it has said what's for sale.

## 2. Hero copy

**The hero image headline is competing with the page's real headline.** The image says "Get Your First Online Income in 90 Days". The description's real opening line is "You don't need an audience. You need something to sell." Those are two different pitches: one is a speed promise, the other is a mechanism. The second is far stronger and far more TDIE.

The expected PROBLEM → DIFFERENT APPROACH → OUTCOME → NEXT STEP order:

- **Problem:** absent from the image. Present and strong in the body ("posted for months with nothing to show for it").
- **Different approach:** only as the sign-off "Build it backwards. Scale it forward.™" at the bottom of the image.
- **Outcome:** "first online income in 90 days". This is a timing claim, and it's generic.
- **Next step:** clear ("START YOUR FREE 7 DAY TRIAL NOW!").

Specific problems:

- **"Proven Strategies · Ready-to-use Templates · Supportive Community · Real Examples"** is the most generic creator-economy line on the page. It could sit on any course. "Real Examples" is also unsupported: the page has one review, and it's about a different product.
- **"No filming. No trending audio. No performing."** is the most specific, most TDIE line in the hero, and it's the smallest text. Keep it and make it bigger.
- **"Build it backwards. Scale it forward.™"** drifts from the trademarked line "Build Your Business Backwards. Scale It Forward.™" Putting ™ on a paraphrase is a trademark-consistency risk. Use the exact mark or drop the ™.
- **"even if you're a complete beginner"** is useful audience qualification. Keep it.

## 3. Positioning

**"Offer first. Funnel second. Automation third. Audience last."** works as a mechanism on this page, not just a slogan, because the paragraph before it names the failure it fixes ("You've been told to build the audience first… posted for months with nothing to show for it"), and the paragraph after it names what the order produces ("an offer, a funnel, an email list that's yours, and a persona that fronts it"). Problem → mechanism → result, in three short lines. **Preserve it exactly.**

Where the positioning breaks:

- **The "Inside" list undercuts it.** "The Offer Edit + The Funnel Edit - turns a following into a business" reintroduces the idea that you need a following, three lines after "You don't need an audience." It's a direct contradiction of the core claim.
- **The tier split undercuts it quietly.** Steps 1 and 2 of the method (offer, funnel) are the Premium modules, per canon §3. So the $9 tier shown in the sidebar doesn't include the first two steps of the order the page sells. The page never says what Standard *does* get you in method terms. That isn't a reason to change tiers, but the copy has to say it plainly (see §5).
- **"Build backwards" is never demonstrated visually on the description.** The site's `/about` page does this with the two-column "usual order / backwards" contrast. If the approved gallery's Before/After image ("Same effort. Opposite order.") is the one to use, it's the missing demonstration. The currently uploaded thumbnail 2 is unreadable at this size, so its copy couldn't be checked.
- **The AI half of the thesis is missing.** Canon's positioning is "AI + architecture". The page carries the architecture and drops the AI.

## 5. Membership decision

**Can a visitor answer "which one is for me?"** No.

- The page never names **Standard** or **Premium** as tiers. It uses "(Premium)" tags on two bullets and nothing else.
- The only prices a visitor sees are **$9/month** (sidebar) and **$35/month** (price-increase line), with no tier attached to either. A cold reader can easily read the $35 line as "this $9 group becomes $35 on 1 October." That is the single most likely misreading on the page (see F2).
- Untagged bullets read as included at $9. Some are (AI Twin, Pinterest, Email, Content, Monetization, all Standard per canon), but "Over 20 Modules Inside" is only true across *both* tiers. Standard alone is roughly 14 including the open courses (canon Decision 94).
- There's no use case for either tier. The site already has approved, canon-accurate use-case lines:
  - Standard: "Everything you need to build the thing and start selling it."
  - Premium: "Standard is what you build by hand. Premium writes your content month for you."

**Premium justification** rests on one line (the calendar). Canon calls the calendar "the primary reason Premium exists over Standard", and the Operating Manual counts ~12 of 1,234 members on Premium. The Offer Edit, Funnel Edit, The Ads Edit™, Scaling & Systems and The Premium Vault (every Value Vault guide unlocked) aren't framed as a group. That group is what Premium buys: *the full build plus the content written for you.*

No pricing changes or new benefits are proposed. All of the above is fixable with the tier contents canon already approves.

Tina's quote was not rewritten and no testimonials were invented.

## 9. Urgency / price increase

Checked against canon §3, Decisions 110 and 114, and the date (Sunday 27 Sep 2026).

- **Does it conflict with other prices?** Yes: the sidebar's $9/month (friction #1). The increase is Premium-only and Standard stays $9, but nothing on the page says so.

"Lock your LOW price in now!" points a *cold* visitor at the flash sale without naming it. **Flagged, not resolved:** is the About page a flash-sale announcement (exception applies), or a public asset (quote $35 only)? That's Jodie's call.
- **Does the urgency help?** The deadline is real and short (canon allows 4–5 day windows), so it isn't fake scarcity. But in line one it's aimed at a stranger who doesn't know what Premium is, so it adds confusion before it adds motivation. Relevance before urgency: move it down and name the tier.
