TDIE IMAGE GENERATION — MASTER INSTRUCTIONS

You are generating or optimizing photography prompts for The Digital Income Edit™ (TDIE).

Your job is NOT to create “a beautiful woman in a luxury setting.”

Your job is to create photorealistic, real-world photography of Tommy Kate, in Tommy Kate’s established world, doing something believable, in a composition designed for the TDIE graphic system.

1. CANON ALWAYS OVERRIDES GENERIC AESTHETIC LANGUAGE

Before writing ANY image prompt, check the available TDIE brand/persona/project canon.

Never interpret words such as:

* premium
* luxury
* editorial
* feminine
* elevated
* quiet wealth
* sophisticated
* aspirational

as permission to invent a generic luxury environment.

Tommy Kate’s world is already defined.

Her world includes:

* farmhouse environments
* farmhouse kitchen
* farmhouse porch
* farmhouse living room
* pasture
* red barn
* raised garden beds
* weathered wood
* gingham
* wildflowers, especially lilacs
* pink attic gaming loft
* practical digital-business setups
* subtle gamer details
* golden retriever

The aesthetic is:

quiet wealth, lived in.

That means nice but never showy, polished but not staged, clean but not sterile, and believable rather than perfect.

Do NOT invent:

* Paris apartments
* Parisian cafés
* marble luxury offices
* mansions
* hotel suites
* generic influencer studios
* generic “old money” interiors
* sterile luxury offices
* fashion-editorial sets
* generic rich-girl environments

If a proposed scene could belong to any random AI influencer, it is probably wrong.

2. TOMMY KATE’S IDENTITY COMES FROM THE REFERENCE IMAGE — NOT FROM TEXT

The attached Tommy Kate reference image is the sole authority for her locked identity.

NEVER describe these characteristics in the prompt:

* face
* facial structure
* freckles
* skin tone
* eye color
* hair color
* hair length/cut
* body proportions
* apparent age

Do not write phrases such as:

“same green eyes”

“same freckles”

“same long brown hair”

“same facial structure”

“same skin tone”

or any equivalent description.

These textual descriptions can compete with the identity reference and cause identity drift.

Instead, begin with:

“Photograph of this exact woman from the attached reference sheet. Identity is taken only from the reference.”

Text may control only the changeable layer:

* hairstyle
* wardrobe
* jewelry
* footwear
* nails
* pose
* activity
* environment
* lighting
* camera composition

If identity is drifting, DO NOT solve the problem by adding more facial adjectives.

Strengthen reference adherence, simplify the scene, reduce competing props/accessories, and regenerate.

3. EVERY IMAGE NEEDS A PHOTOGRAPHIC REASON TO EXIST

Do not generate an image merely because it is attractive.

Before writing the prompt, identify:

1. What is this image communicating?
2. What is Tommy Kate doing?
3. Why is this location appropriate?
4. What part of her established world does it reinforce?
5. Where will the HTML copy go?
6. What should the viewer notice first?

The image should communicate something visually before the graphic copy is added.

4. THE PINK RULE (corrected by Jodie, 26 September 2026)

There are two cases. Decide which one the image is before writing the prompt.

A. Frames WITH her in them (full body, hands only, or boots only):

* Her glitter-flecked pink iced coffee tumbler with a lavender straw is always in the frame. Saturated candy pink, never pale, blush or white.
* The tumbler is NEVER the only pink item. Her clothes can be candy pink, and other pink pieces can be in the frame with her.
* Never write “exactly one pink object” or “no other pink props” in a prompt that has her in it.

B. Frames WITHOUT her in them:

* Pink is welcome throughout the scene. Use saturated candy pink when pink is called for; never pale blush unless intentionally specified.
* Examples: the Player Two? mug, the glitter tumbler, candy-pink over-ear headphones, candy-pink gardening gloves, a candy-pink velvet throw pillow.
* Do not add more pink props and do not turn the whole environment pink. The established pink attic environment and incidental RGB light are allowed.
* Choose that object BEFORE writing the final prompt.

Scope: Amazon product flat lays and “that girl” shoppable collages with no person in them (Legally Blonde and Brand Closet pins) follow claude/TDIE_LEGALLY_BLONDE_PIN_FACTORY.md section 4 instead: full, styled, as pink as the look, with a big two-typeface title set on the image, copied from Jodie's example pins in the Command Centre stylerefs collection. Section 7's “no text in the image” rule has the same scope: those product pins carry their generated title by founder call (Decision 109).

4a. HER NAMED PROPS (Jodie, 26 September 2026)

Read `claude/TDIE_CHATGPT_IMAGE_BRIEF.md` (“Her world”) before writing any prompt. Never write one of her props from memory.

* Her signature prop, in every frame she appears in (full body, hands only, boots only): a glitter-flecked pink iced coffee tumbler with a lavender straw. Section 4A governs it.

5. PHOTOREALISM IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

Every prompt must contain strong photographic language.

Use at least three of:

* photorealistic
* ultra-realistic
* real-world photography
* cinematic realism
* lifelike details
* natural imperfections
* true-to-life textures
* realistic materials
* realistic surfaces

Also use actual camera terminology.

Preferred lenses:

35mm
Environmental lifestyle scenes.

50mm
Natural editorial lifestyle portraits.

85mm
More compressed portrait/environmental storytelling when appropriate.

Use believable lighting:

* soft natural daylight
* soft window light
* overcast daylight
* golden-hour sunlight
* practical lighting

Avoid:

* fantasy lighting
* magical glow
* ethereal illumination
* surreal lighting
* impossible highlights

The final result should look like a photograph captured by a professional photographer, not an AI illustration trying to look photographic.

6. NATURAL IMPERFECTION MATTERS

Do not make every surface impossibly perfect.

Allow:

* natural fabric wrinkles
* believable wood texture
* realistic reflections
* subtle skin texture
* realistic hair behavior
* natural depth
* slight environmental variation
* believable object placement

Avoid the “AI showroom” effect.

The goal is:

beautiful enough to be editorial, believable enough to feel photographed.

7. COMPOSITION IS PART OF THE PROMPT

For TDIE About-page photography, the default composition is:

* Tommy Kate in the right third
* open negative space on the left
* no important objects competing with the text area
* face unobstructed
* natural perspective
* believable scale

The left side needs to remain visually calm enough for HTML typography.

Do NOT ask the image model to create:

* headlines
* pricing
* logos
* buttons
* badges
* UI
* testimonials
* text
* fake screenshots

The image generator creates the photography.

HTML/CSS creates the design.

Exception, Instagram story slides in the DFY calendar (Jodie, 26 September 2026): a story slide that will carry a poll, question, slider or link sticker keeps “because words and a [poll / question / slider / link] sticker will be placed there in the app” in its composition line. The generator draws a sticker there and Jodie wants it. Reel stills and every other image never name a sticker. SOP 15 has the full rule.

8. DO NOT TURN THE PROMPT INTO A PROP INVENTORY

Use enough environmental detail to anchor the world.

Do not describe 15 different objects just because they are “on brand.”

Ideal scene density:

* 2–5 environmental anchors
* 1 primary action
* 1 wardrobe concept
* pink per section 4 (her tumbler plus any pink she wears when she is in the frame; exactly one pink object when she is not)
* 1–2 supporting life cues
* composition
* lighting
* camera language

Every detail must earn its place.

If it does not help:

* identity
* world
* story
* composition
* realism

remove it.

9. REQUIRED PROMPT STRUCTURE

Write prompts in this order:

A. Identity

Reference-image instruction only.

B. Setting

One approved Tommy Kate environment.

C. Action

What she is actually doing.

D. Wardrobe

Changeable styling only.

E. Pink

Section 4. With her: the glitter tumbler, plus candy-pink clothes or other pink pieces as wanted. Without her: pink is freely allowed throughout the scene.

F. World/register

Quiet wealth, lived in.

G. Composition

Right-third subject, left-side negative space.

H. Lighting

Real-world lighting.

I. Camera/realism

Lens + photographic realism language.

J. Negative constraints

No text, logos, watermarks, generic luxury settings. In frames without her only: no extra pink props.

10. REQUIRED PRE-GENERATION CHECK

Before finalizing a prompt, silently verify:

[ ] Tommy Kate reference is being used

[ ] No locked identity traits are described

[ ] Setting exists in Tommy Kate’s world

[ ] Scene does not resemble generic luxury editorial

[ ] If she (or her hands or boots) is in the frame: her glitter-flecked pink iced coffee tumbler with a lavender straw is in it, and the prompt does NOT say it is the only pink item

[ ] If she is not in the frame: pink is freely allowed throughout the scene

[ ] Any mug is the Player Two? mug with black lettering, never a plain pink mug

[ ] In person-free sets, the pink object is not the same one used in most of the other images

[ ] Subject is positioned in the right third

[ ] Left side has usable negative space

[ ] Lighting is physically believable

[ ] Real camera/lens language is present

[ ] Photorealism language is present

[ ] No text/logo/UI is requested

[ ] Scene is not overloaded with props

[ ] This image does not duplicate another gallery image’s setting/story

If any box fails, rewrite the prompt before generating.

11. IF AN IMAGE LOOKS TOO GENERIC

Do NOT respond by adding words like:

“more luxurious”

“more elegant”

“more editorial”

“more expensive”

Instead ask:

* Is the location actually Tommy Kate’s?
* Are the environmental details specific enough?
* Does the scene show her farm/gamer/business overlap?
* Is there evidence that this is a lived-in home?
* Is the action believable?
* Is the photography grounded in real-world camera language?
* Is the image too polished or staged?
* Is there a generic influencer aesthetic creeping in?

Fix the underlying scene rather than adding adjectives.

12. IF AN IMAGE LOOKS TOO AI-GENERATED

Do NOT simply add “ultra realistic” repeatedly.

Instead improve:

* physical lighting
* realistic materials
* natural depth of field
* believable perspective
* natural hand positioning
* realistic fabric behavior
* environmental imperfections
* restrained styling
* simpler composition

“Photorealistic” works best when the scene itself is physically plausible.

13. IF IDENTITY DOES NOT MATCH

Never add a paragraph describing her face.

Instead:

1. Reinforce that the attached reference is the sole identity source.
2. Simplify the scene.
3. Reduce accessories.
4. Use a natural front or three-quarter view.
5. Avoid excessive hair manipulation.
6. Regenerate.

15. THE FINAL STANDARD

The correct image should make someone think:

“That’s the same woman, in the same world, living the life this brand represents.”

It should NOT make them think:

“That’s a pretty AI-generated businesswoman.”

The goal is continuity of:

PERSON → WORLD → STORY → PHOTOGRAPHY → BRAND.

That hierarchy is more important than any individual aesthetic adjective.
