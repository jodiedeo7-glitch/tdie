## 3b. THE REALISM METHOD (adopted 24 Sep 2026 from the "Using AI for mockups" guide by Marketing by Mia, which Jodie trusts)

These rules sit on top of sections 4 and 5 and win where they differ.

1. **The reference photo does most of the work.** Never ask a generator to "make it realistic". Show it what realistic looks like. Every generation gets these attachments: (a) a STYLE reference photo (a real photo in the look we want), (b) the product sheet, (c) for lifestyle shots, the Omni seed.
2. **Style reference library (changed 26 Sep 2026, no folder on Jodie's computer).** Flat lay and collage style references are Jodie's own example pins, stored in the Command Centre database, collection `stylerefs`: `sr-flatlay-grid`, `sr-soft-pink-collage`, `sr-pink-dress-flatlay`, `sr-amazon-fashion-looks-expensive`, `sr-that-girl-amazon-finds-blush`, `sr-that-girl-amazon-finds-script`, `sr-it-girl-text-over-photo` (each doc holds the image as a data string; read it with ArtifactData, decode it to a file in the cloud workspace, attach it). Lifestyle style references are our own published lifestyle photos in the repo, fetched from `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jodiedeo7-glitch/tdie/main/src/lifestyle/<slug>-lifestyle.jpg` (mirror selfie: `pink-suit-law-student-halloween-costume` or `pink-witch-halloween-costume`; walk-away: `pink-graduation-gown-halloween-costume` or `pink-cat-halloween-costume`; chin-down crop: `pink-workwear-freezing-office`). The prompt always says "use it as a style reference, not an exact copy". When Jodie loves one of our pins, add it to `stylerefs`.
3. **Talk to the generator like a designer or photo editor.** Write in plain sentences, not keyword lists. The opening that works: "Using the first image as a style reference, not an exact copy, please make me a very realistic lifestyle shot of [the item(s)] in the other screenshots. I can't use the exact Amazon images outside of Amazon, so it needs to be a new photo." Then describe the setting, outfit, pose, lighting, camera angle and background.
4. **Phone-photo realism.** Every lifestyle prompt includes: "like a good-quality photo taken on a phone, natural light, realistic camera angle, small real-life imperfections." Never studio perfect, never a catalogue.
5. **Get it right in the first prompt.** Write the layout, the full item list and the "everything fully inside the frame" line into the first prompt, so the first generation is usable.
6. **Refine, never restart, and stop early.** If something is off (wrong colour, a missing item, a warped bag strap, an item cut off at the edge), tell the tool EXACTLY what to change, in the same chat. Hard cap: two correction rounds per image. Grain, a tiny stray mark on an insole or a slight crop are fixed by hand in the cloud workspace (section 3c), never with another generation.
7. **Kill the AI shine.** If an image looks glossy, add grain by hand in the cloud workspace (section 3c) instead of another generation.
8. **Faces give it away.** On the lifestyle shot, Tommy Kate's face is hidden or not the focus in most shots. The formats that win in this niche are:
   - a mirror selfie with the phone covering her face;
   - a chin-down outfit crop;
   - an over-the-shoulder walk-away;
   - a detail shot of hands, bag and jewellery.
   The Omni seed is still attached every time, so her build, hair and style stay consistent. Rotate the formats across the week. At most one shot in three shows her full face. When it does, it must look like a candid phone photo. Ask Gemini for "one single photo, not a collage or grid" (on 26 Sep a correction came back as a three-panel strip).
9. **Two flat lay formats.** Alternate the styled flat lay and the "that girl" shoppable collage, both as described in section 4.

## 3c. FAST PATH (tested 26 Sep 2026; use it, it cuts the run time and usage)

- **Browser:** take the lock first (`claude/TDIE_BROWSER_LOCK.md`). Keep at most three tabs of your own. Use JavaScript reads instead of screenshots wherever a read will do. One product or one step per browser batch; long waits inside one script time out.
- **Amazon search (changed 27 Sep 2026):** open each search in the tab and read the loaded results page with JavaScript (skip "Sponsored"; read `data-asin`, the h2 text, `.a-icon-alt` for stars, the aria-label ending in "ratings"). Colour variants: open the product page and read `"dimensionValuesDisplayData"`. Stock and main image: `#availability`, `#landingImage` `data-old-hires` (strip the `/W/.../images` part so it reads `https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/<id>`). Never `fetch()` Amazon pages in the background.
- **SiteStripe short link:** open the product page, wait 3 s, click "Get Link" in the SiteStripe bar, then in JavaScript replace `navigator.clipboard.writeText` with a function that stores the text, click the Full Link radio then the Short Link radio (`input[name=link-type]`), wait 5 s, click `#amzn-ss-copy-affiliate-link-btn-announce`, and read the stored text. It must start with `https://link.amazon/`; if it is the long link, wait and click copy again. Keep collected links in `localStorage` so a frozen tab loses nothing.
- **Product sheet (reference, no downloads):** on an amazon.com tab, add a fixed full-window white overlay holding a 3 by 2 grid (`grid-template-rows: repeat(2, minmax(0,1fr))`) of the look's main images from `https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/<id>`, then take one screenshot with `save_to_disk`. That one file is the product reference for both images. Copy it to `/mnt/user-data/outputs/` so `file_upload` can attach it.
- **Idea List:** storefront page > Create content > Idea List (opens a new tab at `amazon.com/create/collection...`). Type the title and description, click "Add products", open the "Browse history" tab (it has the search box), search one ASIN, tick the result, click Done; repeat per item. Dismiss the AI description suggestion (its X). Save > Submit. The public list URL is read from the storefront page afterwards: `a[href*="/list/"]` with the look's title.
- **Higgsfield (Seedream 4.5):** `https://higgsfield.ai/ai/image?model=seedream_v4_5`. Clear old references by clicking each filled slot's first button. Attach cloud-workspace files with `file_upload` on the empty `input[type=file]`; attach an earlier result by fetching its cloudfront address in the page and setting it on the file input with a `DataTransfer` (then wait 5 s and confirm the slot shows an image before generating). The prompt box is a Lexical editor: set it with `el.__lexicalEditor.setEditorState(editor.parseEditorState(<JSON with one paragraph>))`. Check the Unlimited switch reads `aria-checked="true"`, then click the submit button. Results appear as `https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_39gugAvyvUyyrk2hgob0urVaoIw/hf_<date>_<time>_<id>_min.webp` (full size is the same name ending `.png`). Another session can be using the same account: only trust results whose time is after your own generate click.
- **Gemini:** click "Upload & tools" once, then the `input[type=file]` whose accept is `image/*` takes all attachments in one `file_upload` (style reference, Omni seed as a JPEG under 400 KB, product sheet). Set the prompt by clearing `.ql-editor` and appending one `<p>` with the text, then click "Send message". The result is a `blob:` image of about 687 by 1024 in the page. Model "Flash" still makes images.
- **Getting an image into the cloud workspace (replaces downloading):** show the image in a fixed overlay at an exact CSS size and capture it with the `zoom` action, `scale: 1`, `save_to_disk: true`; the saved file is at CSS pixel size. Higgsfield results: open the cloudfront address in its own tab (that tab is at 100% zoom), show it at 1000 by 1500 and capture two tiles (top at 0 and at 637 CSS pixels, region `[0, 0, 820, 708]`), then stitch to 1000 by 1500. Gemini results: overlay the `blob:` image inside the Gemini tab at 838 by 1249, capture tiles at 0 and 382, stitch, resize to 1000 by 1500. Gemini's page blocks outside images, so view cloudfront images in their own tab, not in Gemini.
- **Hand fixes in the cloud workspace:** grain (add light noise, sigma about 5), small crops that keep 2:3, and removing a tiny stray mark with an inpaint over a tight mask. These never need another generation.

## 4. PIN ONE: THE FLAT LAY OR "THAT GIRL" COLLAGE (rewritten 26 Sep 2026 from Jodie's example pins)

Jodie's verdict on the 26 Sep collage ("pink for a cold office", white background, six items in two neat boxes, small title): **"WAY too plain."** Plain white or empty backgrounds, lots of empty space, items sitting in tidy boxes, and a small title on a cream band are all out. What they share: the frame is full and abundant, edge to edge; the items overlap and touch; the outfit is surrounded by styling extras that make it feel like a real girl's things; the background has colour or texture; and the title is big, sits on the image itself, and mixes a bold serif with a thick script.

Tool routing (no person in frame): **Seedream 4.5 on Higgsfield, Unlimited toggle ON, 2K, portrait 2:3** (changed from 4K on 27 Sep 2026: the pin is saved at 1000 by 1500, so 4K detail was discarded), with a `stylerefs` image and the product sheet attached. **Colour:** these product pins are as pink as the look, like the examples. The one-saturated-candy-pink-object rule does not apply to these (scoped by Jodie's 26 Sep examples).

### Format A: the styled flat lay (style refs `sr-pink-dress-flatlay`, `sr-flatlay-grid`)
A real overhead photo. The outfit is laid out as if worn (top above bottom, shoes at the hem, bag at the hip, jewellery at the neckline), pieces overlapping and touching. It sits on a textured surface that suits the look: fluffy pink faux fur, rumpled pink satin, crumpled white linen, a chunky knit throw, warm wood floorboards or velvet. Styling extras are tucked in around it: a small bunch of fresh flowers (tulips, peonies, roses), a latte or iced coffee, sunglasses, an unlabelled perfume bottle, a lip gloss, a hair bow or claw clip, a candle, a small instant camera or a book. 10 to 16 objects in total, including the 5 to 8 products. Almost no empty background. Soft natural window light with real shadows.

### Format B: the "that girl" shoppable collage (style refs `sr-that-girl-amazon-finds-blush`, `sr-that-girl-amazon-finds-script`, `sr-amazon-fashion-looks-expensive`, `sr-soft-pink-collage`)
Each product as a clean cut-out with a soft drop shadow, packed around the title on a soft coloured background (blush pink, dusty blue, cream, pale lilac, or softly textured paper or fabric). Items overlap slightly and fill the canvas edge to edge, with extra matching finds tucked in: a beauty item or two, rings, a hair bow. A few tiny accents: small hearts, sparkles, a little bow, a hand-drawn arrow, or one small circular inset detail photo.

### THE TEXT (both formats, copy the examples)
- The title sits **on the image**, across the middle or the upper third. No separate band or box.
- It is a **two-typeface lockup:** a bold, high-contrast serif (often capitals, like "AMAZON FASHION") paired with a thick flowing script word or phrase (like "looks expensive" or "that girl"). Or big stacked serif lines over the photo with one word in script or italic, like the "it girl" example.
- 3 to 5 words (canon overlay limit). The lockup fills about half to two thirds of the pin's width.
- Colour: near-black, deep plum or white, whichever contrasts most with what is behind it. One word may be hot pink.
- **Crisp rule still applies (Jodie's standing preference):** the serif is bold or semibold, never a hairline or thin display serif; the script is a thick brush script, never a thin wedding script. Every letter checked at feed size.
- Title lockup examples: "PINK WITCH / costume", "that girl / office edit", "AMAZON FINDS / cold office", "the pink cat / costume", "3 PINK COATS / for Boston".
