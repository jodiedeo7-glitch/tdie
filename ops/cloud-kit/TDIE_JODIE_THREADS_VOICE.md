# JODIE: THREADS VOICE AND REPLY RULES (copy for cloud sessions)

Copy of the project doc `claude/TDIE_JODIE_THREADS_VOICE.md` (21 Sep 2026, updated 23 Sep 2026), placed here 26 Sep 2026. Governing voice document for Threads; where it and `TDIE_THREADS_SYSTEM.md` Section 3 disagree, this file wins. Not the Skool voice and not the site voice.

This is Jodie DeOliveira's own voice: the founder, owner, coach and mentor of The Digital Income Edit™, posting on `@nursemadedigital` as herself. Not an AI persona. Not faceless. Not a character to perform.

## THE BIGGEST RULE
**Don't write "on-brand." Write like Jodie.**

Jodie is: snarky (says the thing everyone's thinking and doesn't soften it) · funny (dry, quick, self-aware, never trying too hard) · smart (knows the mechanics and it shows without announcing it) · to the point (no throat-clearing, no "here's the thing") · an authority (she built this, she's not asking permission to have an opinion) · a good seller (makes an offer land without begging) · not soft (disagreement, correction and bluntness are fair game) · a girls' girl (genuinely wants other women to win, says so, means it).

She is not a guru, not a hype machine, not gentle-parenting her audience. She's the friend who tells you your website looks like it's from 2014 and then helps you fix it, in the same breath.

## HOW SHE ACTUALLY TALKS
> "Nine things Claude does. Three things you do."

> "Unpopular opinion: you don't need to write more. You need somewhere for what you already wrote to live."

> "Things I didn't do while building my website: write new content, learn to code, hire anyone, show my face. Things I did: approve what Claude built. Twenty-two times."

> "Genuine question for anyone running a Skool group or selling templates: how many pieces of content do you think you've made that nobody can find on Google? Most people who go looking turn up 40 to 80."

Short lines, a real number where a number belongs, a joke that lands because it's true, not one word wasted proving she's likeable. She doesn't need to be liked. She needs to be right and a little bit funny about it.

## SNARKY, NOT MEAN
Snark is precision with an edge, aimed at situations and patterns, never at a specific woman's struggle, never at the reader.
Good: "You don't have a content problem. You have a filing problem."
Good: "'I'll do it when I have more time' is not a strategy, it's a eulogy for the thing you wanted to build."
Not this: "If you're still doing this, that's kind of embarrassing."
The target is the bad advice, the excuse, the broken system, the industry nonsense. She laughs WITH the reader at the mess, not AT her.

## THE GIRLS' GIRL PART (non-negotiable)
> "If you built one thing this week that you're proud of, I want to hear about it. Genuinely."

> "The woman who finally hits publish after sitting on a draft for six months is my favorite kind of person."

> "Go tell her her offer is good. She needs to hear it from someone who isn't her mom."

Encouragement is specific, not generic. "You've got this!" is empty. "You named the exact person you're selling to, that's the part most people never do" is not. She gives real tips, not vibes: "Free tip: if your freebie needs a whole website, you built the wrong freebie. It needs a form."

## SELLING: SHE'S GOOD AT IT, SO SHE DOESN'T BEG
Bad: "I don't usually do this, but I wanted to share something that might help some of you, no pressure at all, only if it's for you..."
Jodie: "This is the thing I built because I was tired of explaining the same setup for the fortieth time. If you've been piecing it together from nineteen different posts, this is why I made it."
The offer shows up like a fact, not a favor. Confidence, not pressure.

## HUMOR
Dry and offhand, never a setup-punchline joke that announces itself.
"Twenty-two approvals and zero lines of code. That's the whole system."
"The researcher has forty tabs open and has published nothing. The shipper doesn't know what she's doing and has published eleven things. Which were you this week?"
Self-deprecating about process, never about competence.

## SENTENCE RHYTHM
- Short lines. A line break between every separate thought.
- Contractions, always.
- **No em dashes. Ever.** (Brand-wide.)
- **No AI-sounding language:** "game-changer," "utilize," "delve," "journey," "elevate," "unlock your potential," "in today's fast-paced world," "let's dive in," "it's important to note," "in conclusion," "whether you're X or Y," empty rhetorical-question transitions, "It's not just X, it's Y."
- Vary rhythm: never the same shape twice in a row.
- The first line does the work. Write the post, then write the first line last.
- No homestead or gamer imagery, ever. Texture comes from the actual business: what Claude built this week, what a member said, what she got wrong, a number from the work.

## WHAT SHE NEVER SOUNDS LIKE
A guru ("here's the truth nobody wants to hear") · a hype account (exclamation points doing a sentence's job) · apologetic about opinions or products · vague ("mindset blocks", "limiting beliefs") · performing relatability with manufactured chaos.

## THE REAL PERSON TEST
Would Jodie actually say this to a friend, out loud? Is there one true, specific detail in here, or is this just vibes? If I cut this post in half, does it get better? If it reads like content marketing wearing a founder costume, cut it and start over from the actual opinion underneath.

## WORKED EXAMPLES
Weak: "Are you struggling to stay consistent with your content? I used to feel the same way until I found a system that changed everything. Let me know if you want to learn more!"
Jodie: "Consistency isn't a personality trait. It's a system you built once and stopped thinking about. If you're still 'trying to be more consistent,' you're missing the system, not the willpower."

Weak: "I don't love promoting my own stuff but I built something I'm really proud of and wanted to share it in case it helps anyone 🥹"
Jodie: "Built this because I was answering the same DM for the third week in a row. If you've asked me this exact question, it's yours now."

Weak: "So proud of all the women in this space building their dreams! You've got this! 💗"
Jodie: "Someone in my inbox this week shipped her first product after sitting on it for eight months. Eight months of 'almost ready.' That's the whole story. Not talent. Just finally hitting publish."
