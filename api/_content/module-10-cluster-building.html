A pillar alone doesn't rank. A pillar with a cluster does.

Supporting articles are shorter — 800 to 1,500 words — and each one answers a single specific question inside the pillar's territory. Each links up to the pillar. The pillar links down to several of them.

That structure is what a search engine reads as authority. One deep article says you wrote something good. A pillar with eight supporting articles all linking to it says you cover this subject.

This is also where the rest of your inventory goes. Those forty leftover rows aren't scraps — they're the cluster.

01

Find your cluster topics

Copy this prompt
Here is my pillar article on [TOPIC]. Give me 12 supporting
article topics that would form a cluster around it.

For each: the working title, the specific search question it
answers, and roughly how long it needs to be.

Rules:
- Each must answer one question the pillar raises but doesn't
  fully resolve
- No two may target the same search intent
- None may be broad enough to compete with the pillar itself

Now match them against your inventory. Most will already have a source — a lesson, a post, a DM. Those get written first.

02

The supporting article prompt

Copy this prompt
Write the supporting article "[TITLE]" for the [PILLAR] cluster.
Source material above. Canon File for products, prices, voice.

LIVE FACTS:
- Publishes to: /learn/[slug]
- Parent pillar: [title] at [url]
- Category: [category]
- Free resource: [name] at [url] — or NONE
- Paid product: [exact name and price] — or GAP, omit

Requirements:
- 800-1,500 words. Focused, not comprehensive.
- Answers exactly one question. Resist scope creep.
- Links to the parent pillar in context, naturally, in the first
  third — not a bare "read more" at the bottom
- Answer-first paragraphs under every subhead

Same rules as the pillar: no invented products, no padding,
no hype vocabulary. Complete article in markdown.

How many, and when to stop

Minimum viable cluster: 5 supporting articles. Below that it reads as a page, not a topic.

Strong cluster: 8–12.

When to stop: when new topics start overlapping with ones you've written. Two articles chasing the same search phrase split your own signal and you get outranked by someone with one good page.

The number that tells you to stop entirely: a pillar still under 3 supporting articles a year after launch. That's not a content backlog, that's a topic you don't actually want to write about. Cut it and put the effort into a pillar that's pulling.

If you get stuck
I don't have source material for half of these

Write the ones you do. A cluster of five real articles beats twelve where seven are generic. Missing sources are also useful signal — a topic you have nothing to say about probably isn't your topic.

These two articles overlap

Merge into one better article. Two thin overlapping pages hurt each other.

Before you move on

A cluster plan for your first pillar, and 2–3 supporting articles live.
