Four stages, and each piece of your site has exactly one job in moving someone along:

Article → answers their question completely, asks nothing Free resource → trades something useful for an email Paid product → the thing that solves it properly Community → where they stay

Most self-built sites fail by asking for all four at once. Every page pushes the newsletter, the product, the community, and three related articles. The reader chooses nothing, because a page with four asks reads as a page with no answer.

The rule

One primary ask per page. Everything else is secondary and looks it.

  • Pillar articles → primary ask is the free resource. They just arrived; they don't trust you enough to buy.
  • Supporting articles → primary ask is the pillar. Deepen the visit first.
  • Resource pages → primary ask is the signup. Nothing else on the page.
  • Product pages → primary ask is the purchase. No newsletter link competing with it.
01

Where CTAs go

In the body, once, at natural relevance. When the article reaches the moment where the resource genuinely helps, link it there. This position outperforms the bottom substantially, because it catches people at interest rather than at exit.

In the journey block, from Module 11. Consistent, automatic, template-driven.

Not in a popup. Not on a self-built site with new traffic. You'd be interrupting someone in their first thirty seconds with you.

Not in a sidebar. Most of your traffic is on a phone, where sidebars collapse to the bottom and are never seen.

02

The paid slot honestly

Some pillars won't have a matching product. From your Module 3 pillar map, those say GAP.

Leave them empty. Do not substitute your nearest product. A reader who finishes an article on email sequences and gets pitched a Pinterest course learns that your recommendations aren't about them. That costs more than the missed sale.

GAP is also your roadmap. The pillar pulling the most traffic with an empty paid slot is the next thing you should build.

03

Never put two paid offers side by side

If you have multiple products or tiers, don't present them as a comparison on a content page. Comparison invites deliberation, and deliberation on a blog article ends in leaving.

One offer, the right one for that reader, at that moment. Comparison belongs on a pricing page, where they arrived already deciding.

If you get stuck
I have no paid product at all

Build the site anyway. Every article routes to the free resource, you build the list, and the list tells you what to sell. This is a better sequence than guessing at a product first.

Which product for a pillar that fits two?

The cheaper one, if it's a real solution. Someone who buys a $27 thing and it works buys the $197 thing later. Someone who's pitched $197 first buys nothing.

Before you move on

Every pillar routed: one free resource, one paid product or an honest empty slot.
