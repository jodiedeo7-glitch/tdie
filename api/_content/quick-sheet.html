Work through EVERY item in these areas, oldest to newest. Do not skip any:

2. My own community posts — filter to posts by me
3. Any call recordings or replays listed
4. Any files, PDFs, or downloads attached anywhere

For each item, append a row to a running table with these columns:
Source | Title | Topic | Format | Approx length | Key points covered

"Key points covered" should be 2-3 short bullets on what the item
actually teaches — enough that I can tell later whether it's worth
building an article from.

Rules:
- Read-only. Do not edit, delete, publish, or send anything.
- Do not skip short items. A four-sentence post is a row.
- If something is too long to fully load, note it as PARTIAL rather
  than guessing at the contents.

  and give me the complete table at the end.
- If you approach the response limit, stop at a clean row and end
  with "PAUSED AT [row number]". I will say continue.

- No free trial
- No annual plan
- No 1:1 coaching
- [every plausible thing you don't offer]

HARD RULES
1. Never invent a product, price, link, or CTA. Not in the table above = does not exist.
2. If something is missing, stop and ask. Do not fill the gap.
3. Full trademark name on first reference.
4. When two sources conflict, stop and flag it. Never resolve it silently.

VOICE
Sounds like: [three descriptors]
Never say: hack, secret, explode, crush it, game-changer, [your additions]
Register: [e.g. warm and direct in community, editorial on the blog]

STRUCTURE
URLs: /[pattern]/[slug]
Stack: [your stack]
Colors: [hex codes]
Fonts: [names]

Image prompt anatomy

The nine parts every image prompt needs. Leave one out and results go random.

Copy this prompt
1. Subject — what's in frame
2. Setting — where
3. Lighting — "soft natural window light" / "golden hour"
4. Camera — "Canon R5, 85mm f/1.4, shallow depth of field"
5. Palette — exact hex codes, every time
6. Text — exact words in quotes, with placement
7. Composition — where the subject sits, where text goes
8. Realism — "a real photograph, not a 3D render, not CGI"
9. Format — "vertical 2:3" for pins
