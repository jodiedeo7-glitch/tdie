The idea

You need images constantly — pin graphics, article headers, resource covers, module covers. You don't need to be in any of them, and you don't need a photographer.

Two tools, two jobs:

AI image generation — photographic scenes, lifestyle images, anything with a person in it.

Canva — text-heavy graphics, and as a correction layer over AI images when the rendered text comes out misspelled.

Do this — the anatomy of a prompt that works

Nine parts. Include all nine and the results stop being random:

  1. Subject — what's in frame
  2. Setting — where
  3. Lighting — "soft natural window light," "golden hour"
  4. Camera — "Canon R5, 85mm f/1.4, shallow depth of field." This does real work; it's the difference between a photograph and an illustration.
  5. Palette — your exact hex codes, every time
  6. Text — the exact words, in quotes, with placement
  7. Composition — where the subject sits, where text goes
  8. Realism enforcement — "a real photograph, not a 3D render, not CGI"
  9. Format — "vertical 2:3" for pins

Do this — the consistency problem

Generate three covers separately and you get three different brands. Slightly different lighting, different color temperature, different mood. Individually fine. Side by side in a classroom, obviously unrelated.

The fix is a locked shell. Write one prompt where everything is fixed except a few named swap fields. Change only those.

LOCKED SHELL — change ONLY the swap fields. Every other word
stays identical across every cover.

A real photograph, not a 3D render, not CGI, of [SUBJECT],
[POSE], in [FIXED SETTING] with soft natural window light.
Canon R5, 85mm f/1.4, shallow depth of field.

Subject in the right third of frame. Text occupies the left
two-thirds, using that space generously — no empty dead area,
no floating text island.

Headline "[HEADLINE]" in [HEX], large enough to dominate the
left panel. Subtitle "[SUBTITLE]" beneath in [HEX].

A solid strip runs the full bottom edge, substantial in height,
fully opaque. Strip color: [BADGE HEX]. Label "[BADGE]" on it.

Where the text panel meets the photograph, a soft gradient
blend — no hard vertical seam.

Colors rendered rich and true-to-tone, not desaturated.

Palette: [your hex codes]. PHOTOREALISM — enforce hard.
Vertical format.

SWAP FIELDS: [SUBJECT] [POSE] [HEADLINE] [SUBTITLE] [BADGE]
[BADGE HEX]

Every specific instruction in that shell exists because its absence produced a specific failure: washed-out color, a hard panel seam, dead space, a pale undersized headline, a translucent strip, a CGI look.

Do this — a consistent person without a real person

If you want a recognizable face across your brand that isn't yours, generate one and then never describe it in words again.

Word descriptions drift. "A woman in her thirties with long brown hair" produces a different woman every time.

Instead: generate one image you're happy with, save it as your reference, and attach it to every future generation, referring to it as "this exact person." Describe only what changes — the setting, the clothes, the pose.

Lock the wardrobe too. A short list of things she wears keeps the brand coherent. A recurring prop helps more than it sounds like it should.

Do this — text in images

AI image tools render text well now, but not perfectly. Occasionally you get a misspelling in a headline.

Always check the text before publishing. When it's wrong, don't regenerate and hope — open the image in Canva and lay correct text over it. Faster and certain.

The hard ban

No trademarked characters, brands, logos, or IP in any generated image.

Not as a background element, not as a joke, not "in the style of" a known franchise. This has cost people their accounts on platforms that don't offer appeals. There is no version of this that's worth it.

Roadblock — "My images look like 3D renders"

Add "a real photograph, not a 3D render, not CGI" and add real camera specs. Both, not one.

Roadblock — "Colors don't match my brand"

Hex codes in every prompt, every time. Color names aren't precise enough — "pink" spans a hundred colors and you'll get a different one each generation.

Roadblock — "Text is unreadable over the photo"

Ask for text in a distinct panel or over a solid area, not floating over the photograph. Then blend the panel edge into the image so it doesn't read as two pasted rectangles.

Deliverable

A locked shell prompt for your brand, a reference image if you're using a consistent person, and your first batch of pin graphics.
