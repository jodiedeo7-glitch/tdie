A pillar article is the deep, definitive piece on one of your topics. It's long — 3,000 words is the floor, not the target — and it exists to be the best thing on the internet about that specific question.

Long-form works here for a reason people misstate. It isn't that Google counts words. It's that a genuinely complete answer takes 3,000 words, and a reader who finds a complete answer stops going back to the search results. That behavior is what gets measured. Padding a thin article to 3,000 words does nothing, because the reader still leaves.

Your Skool lesson is the spine. It's already the real method. What it lacks is context a stranger needs — the lesson was written for someone who already joined, already trusts you, and already read the three lessons before it. A stranger arriving from Google has none of that.

Claude fills that gap. You supply the method.

01

Pull your source material

Skool lessons: open the lesson, select all, copy. Include your own comments underneath — you often clarified something there that belongs in the article.

Call recordings: get a transcript. Most recording tools generate one; if not, upload the audio to Claude and ask for a transcript. Raw transcripts are messy and that's fine — you're using it as source, not text.

Community posts: copy the post and the replies. The replies tell you what confused people, which tells you what the article must address.

02

The pillar article prompt

New conversation in your project. Paste your source material first, then this:

Copy this prompt
Write the pillar article for [PILLAR TOPIC]. The source material
above is my actual method — it's the spine of the article. Use my
Canon File in project knowledge for products, prices, links, and voice.

LIVE FACTS — override anything that disagrees:
- URL this publishes to: /learn/[slug]
- Category: [category]
- Free resource for this pillar: [name] at [url] — or NONE
- Paid product for this pillar: [exact name], [exact price],
  at [url] — or GAP, omit the slot entirely
- Related articles: [three, with URLs] — or NONE YET

Requirements:
- 3,000 words minimum. This is a floor.
- Chapter structure with descriptive subheads
- Every chapter opens with a paragraph that directly answers the
  question in its subhead — answer first, elaboration after
- Written for someone who has never heard of me and arrived from
  a search result
- Include an FAQ section at the end, 5-7 real questions
- Concrete and specific. Numbers, examples, actual steps.

Rules:
- Never invent a product, price, or link. If a slot above says GAP
  or NONE, omit that slot rather than substituting something.
- Do not pad. If a section is thin, the fix is more specificity,
  not more sentences.
- No hype vocabulary. Follow the banned list in my Canon File.
- Return the complete article in markdown. Do not summarize what
  you wrote or suggest next steps.

Why "answer first" matters

Open every chapter with the answer, then explain.

Two things read your article: a person deciding within seconds whether this page has what they need, and a search engine looking for a clean, extractable answer to a question. Both want the answer at the top of the section. Both punish a paragraph that warms up for four sentences before saying anything.

This one structural habit does more for readability and search performance than any other single change.

03

Convert to a page

Ask in the same conversation:

Copy this prompt
Convert that article into a complete .astro file at
src/pages/learn/[slug].astro, using ArticleLayout with all props
filled — title, description, category, publishDate, related.

Export the same title, description and category from the file's
frontmatter as well, so the /learn index picks it up automatically.

Complete file, ready to paste.

Commit it to src/pages/learn/. Vercel deploys it in about ninety seconds. It's live.

On images: leave featuredImage off for now. Module 18 covers making them and where the files go. An article without one still ranks; an article pointing at an image that doesn't exist shows a broken box.

If you get stuck
It came out at 1,800 words

Say: This is under the 3,000 floor. Expand by adding depth, not padding — more specific examples, more of the actual how, more edge cases. Don't restate what's already there. Naming what you don't want matters; asking only for "longer" gets you restatement.

It doesn't sound like me

Your Canon File voice section is too vague. The banned word list fixes this faster than any description. Add ten words you'd never say, re-upload, regenerate.

It mentioned a product I don't sell

A Canon File gap. Add it to the DOES NOT EXIST list, re-upload, regenerate. Fixing only the output means it recurs.

Claude won't build it — it's asking me questions

That's correct behavior and Module 22 covers it in full. Something you specified is missing or contradictory. Answer the question; don't override the stop.
