A blog isn't a writing project. It's a retrieval system for work you've already done.

Every question you answered in your community is a paragraph. Every classroom lesson is a section. Every call is three posts. It's written — it's just trapped somewhere nobody can search, on a platform you don't own.

That's the real cost of keeping everything in Skool. Not that Skool is bad; it's excellent at what it does. But nothing inside it ranks, nothing works while you sleep, and a member has to already know you exist to reach any of it.

01

Let Chrome do the collecting

This is the part everyone dreads, and it's the part you're not going to do by hand.

Opening every lesson, every post, every product page and every old broadcast — copying titles, noting what's in each one, building the list — is an afternoon of clicking. It's also the single most common place people quit, because it feels like work before the work.

A browser agent does it while you make coffee. You point it at a platform, walk away, and come back to a finished table.

Use Claude in Chrome, or any browser agent you have. Run it once per platform.

Run 1 — your community or course platform

Copy this prompt
Log into [Skool / Kajabi / Teachable / your platform] and open
[your group or workspace].

Work through EVERY item in these areas, oldest to newest. Do not
skip any:
1. The classroom — every module, every lesson
2. My own posts — filter to posts by me
3. Any call recordings or replays listed
4. Any files, PDFs, or downloads attached anywhere

For each item, append a row to a running table with these columns:
Source | Title | Topic | Format | Approx length | Key points covered

"Key points covered" should be 2-3 short bullets on what the item
actually teaches — enough that I can tell later whether it's worth
building an article from.

Rules:
- Read-only. Do not edit, delete, publish, or send anything.
- Do not skip short items. A four-sentence post is a row.
- If something is too long to fully load, note it as PARTIAL rather
  than guessing at the contents.
- Do not stop between items to ask questions. Run straight through
  and give me the complete table at the end.
- If you approach the response limit, stop at a clean row and end
  with "PAUSED AT [row number]". I will say continue.

Run 2 — your storefront

Copy this prompt
Log into [Beacons / Etsy / Gumroad / Stan / your storefront] and
open my shop.

List EVERY product, including unlisted, draft, and retired ones.
For each, append a row to a running table:
Source | Product name | Price | What it teaches or contains |
Format | Any description copy worth reusing

Then, for any product with a description, sales page, or included
PDF I can view, add 2-3 bullets on the actual content inside it.

Rules:
- Read-only. Do not edit, unpublish, reprice, or delete anything.
- Include free products and lead magnets.
- Run straight through without asking questions. Give me the
  complete table at the end.

Run 3 — your existing website, if you have one

Copy this prompt
Go to [yourdomain.com] and crawl every page you can reach from the
navigation and the sitemap.

For each page, append a row to a running table:
URL | Page title | Type (sales page, blog post, about, freebie) |
Word count estimate | 2-3 bullets on what it covers

Flag any page that is thin, outdated, or duplicated elsewhere.

Read-only. Do not attempt to log in or edit anything. Run straight
through and give me the complete table at the end.

Run 4 — your sent emails

Copy this prompt
Log into [MailerLite / ConvertKit / Flodesk / your email platform]
and open my sent campaigns.

Work through every broadcast I have sent, oldest to newest. For
each, append a row to a running table:
Date | Subject line | Topic | Approx length | 2-3 bullets on what
it actually said

Skip pure promotional emails with no teaching in them — note them
as PROMO ONLY rather than detailing them.

Read-only. Do not send, schedule, edit, or delete anything. Run
straight through and give me the complete table at the end.

Four runs, roughly twenty minutes of unattended time, and you have every asset you own in one place. Doing that by hand is most of a day and it's the reason most people never start.

02

The parts Chrome can't reach

Your DMs. No agent should be reading your inbox. Search your own long replies manually — this is often the best material you have, because explaining something to one stuck person produces clearer writing than "content mode" ever does. Ten minutes.

Call recordings. Log the recording as a row. Phase 2 covers pulling transcripts; you don't need them tonight.

Anything abandoned. Half-finished courses, drafted modules, the outline in your Notes app.

03

Put it where Claude can use it

The table is useless sitting in a browser tab.

1. Save it as a file. content-inventory.md, or a spreadsheet — either works.

2. Create a Claude Project. In Claude, click Projects, then Create project. Name it after your brand. Everything you build this weekend happens inside this one project.

3. Upload the inventory to project knowledge. Open the project, find project knowledge, upload the file.

That's the step that makes the rest of the weekend fast. Every conversation in that project can now see your entire content library without you pasting anything. Your Canon File joins it in Module 2.

Uploads don't sync. If you update the inventory later, delete the old file from project knowledge and upload the new one. Same filename does not mean same file.

What normal looks like

Most people find 40–80 pieces and are startled. Six months of an active Skool group makes 40 a low number.

Under 15 means you're being too strict, not that you're short on content. A four-sentence comment reply is a row.

If you get stuck
It's disorganized and not good enough to publish

It isn't supposed to be. It's raw material. Claude does the structuring and polishing in Phase 2 — that's the point of the build. What Claude can't invent is your actual method and the specific way you explain the thing you're good at. Log it messy.

Chrome only got through half of it

Say continue. Browser agents hit response length limits, not reading limits — it read everything and ran out of room writing it out. If it restarts from the top instead, tell it to resume at a specific row and not repeat earlier ones.

Before you move on

A spreadsheet of everything you own. One row each.
