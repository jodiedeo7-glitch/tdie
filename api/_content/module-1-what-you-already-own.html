The idea

A blog isn't a writing project. It's a retrieval system for work you've already done.

Every question you answered in your community is a paragraph. Every classroom lesson is a section. Every call is three posts. It's written — it's just trapped somewhere nobody can search, on a platform you don't own.

That's the real cost of keeping everything in Skool. Not that Skool is bad; it's excellent at what it does. But nothing inside it ranks, nothing works while you sleep, and a member has to already know you exist to reach any of it.

Do this

Open a blank spreadsheet. Five columns: Source · Title · Topic · Format · Length.

Log everything. Don't judge, don't edit, don't skip things that aren't good enough — quality decisions happen in Module 3.

Where to look:

Your Skool classroom. Every lesson is a row. A lesson is usually a supporting article; a module is usually a pillar.

Your community posts. Filter to your own, sort by most-liked. High engagement means a real problem got solved — pre-validated by the exact audience you want.

Your call recordings. Log the call, not the topics. You'll mine them later.

Your PDFs and freebies. Including the dead ones. Especially those — a freebie nobody downloaded is usually good content behind a bad landing page.

Your emails. Any broadcast you put real thought into.

Your DMs and comment replies. The one people skip, and often the best material you have. Explaining something to one stuck person produces clearer writing than "content mode" ever does. Search your DMs for your own long replies.

Anything you abandoned. Half-finished courses, drafted modules, the outline in your Notes app.

What normal looks like

Most people find 40–80 pieces and are startled. Six months of an active Skool group makes 40 a low number.

Under 15 means you're being too strict, not that you're short on content. A four-sentence comment reply is a row.

Roadblock — "It's disorganized and not good enough to publish"

It isn't supposed to be. It's raw material. Claude does the structuring and polishing in Phase 2 — that's the point of the build. What Claude can't invent is your actual method and the specific way you explain the thing you're good at. Log it messy.

Roadblock — "I have recordings but no transcripts"

Log the recording. Phase 2 covers transcripts.

Deliverable

A spreadsheet of everything you own. One row each.
