Read this part twice

This is the module that separates a website from a website system, and it's worth more than any individual page you'll ever build.

Two files control your entire site:

PageLayout.astro — standalone pages. Homepage, about, resources.

ArticleLayout.astro — every blog article. All of them.

Every article page runs through ArticleLayout. Its header, footer, fonts, colors, email capture, related-article block, and search-engine markup all live in that one file.

Which means: change one file, change every article at once. Forty articles, one edit.

Without this, adding an email signup to your blog is forty edits and you'll miss three. With it, it's one edit, and it's on every article you publish from then on — including the ones you haven't written yet.

The rule that follows from it

If something appears on more than one page, it belongs in the layout, not the page.

Violate this once and you get the failure that costs the most time in the whole build: a change that has to be made in forty places, discovered six months in, when the pages have drifted apart enough that they're not even consistent to begin with.

Do this — the ArticleLayout prompt

New conversation, same project:

Build src/layouts/ArticleLayout.astro. This layout handles every
article on the site. Use my Canon File for brand specifics.

It takes these props:
- title, description, category, publishDate
- featuredImage (optional)
- related (an array of {title, url} — passed in per article,
  never hardcoded in the layout)

It must include:
1. <head>: title, meta description, canonical URL, Open Graph tags
2. Article schema markup (JSON-LD), generated from the props
3. Nav and footer matching PageLayout
4. Article body slot with brand typography
5. An email capture block — as a LINK to [your opt-in URL],
   not an inline form. If I don't have that URL yet, use the
   constant NEWSLETTER_URL with the value "#" so it's a single
   line to change later.
6. A related-articles block rendered from the `related` prop.
   Module 11 expands this into a full journey block — build it
   as one section so it can be replaced cleanly.

Rules:
- If publishDate is missing, omit it from the schema. Never
  generate a fake date.
- If `related` is empty, render nothing rather than a broken block.
- Complete file, ready to paste.

Why the email block is a link, not a form

This is a hard-won one.

A form on your page needs somewhere to send the data. Wire it wrong and it looks perfect — styled, on-brand, submit button and all — and captures nothing. No error, no warning. It just quietly does nothing on every page of your site, for however long it takes you to notice.

The way people usually notice is by testing their own signup months later and finding an empty list.

Linking to your email platform's hosted opt-in page removes the failure entirely. Their page, their form, their infrastructure. Slightly less elegant, permanently reliable.

You may not have that URL yet. Module 12 sets up your email platform. Until then set the constant to "#" — the block renders and you change one line on Saturday night.

Put that URL in one constant at the top of the layout:

const NEWSLETTER_URL = "https://your-opt-in-page-url";

Change platforms later and you edit one line, once.

Do this — the two dates rule

Google needs a publish date to show your article as a rich result in search. Two rules:

  1. Pass a real publishDate on every article.
  2. Never invent one. If you don't know it, leave it out — the schema omits it and you lose a rich result. Faking it means Google can catch the mismatch, and that costs more than the rich result was worth.

Roadblock — "Do I need both layouts?"

Yes, and they should share a nav and footer. If you find yourself editing nav links in two places, ask Claude to extract the nav into a shared component. Same principle, one level up.

Roadblock — "My four standalone pages each have their own header"

Common when pages get built individually before the layout exists. Every one is a separate edit for every future change. Fix it now: Refactor these pages to use PageLayout instead of their own HTML head and nav. Give me each file complete.

Deliverable

ArticleLayout.astro and PageLayout.astro committed, sharing a nav and footer, with the newsletter URL in a single constant.
